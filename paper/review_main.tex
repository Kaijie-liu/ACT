\begin{abstract}
A mixture-of-experts (MoE) program may preserve its output property while its
route changes under perturbation. We organize verification over all tie-legal
routing histories and reduce guarded, weighted output obligations to static
neural verification problems. The reductions connect complete route coverage,
output-side guard specifications, and an independent-gate relaxation with exact
last-gate endpoint obligations. We evaluate these constructions with a strong
nonlinear branch-and-bound engine rather than assuming a specialized abstract
representation is necessary. In a 180-object multilayer comparison, a pruned
frontend obtains 108 numerical positives, while naive enumeration and our
initial HybridZ implementation obtain none. A subsequent 300-object paired
comparison gives 122 positives with a gate-decoupling cascade versus 116
without it: nine gains, three losses, and a net two percentage points with an
input-block interval of [0,4]. This fails the registered five-point practical
threshold. A revised, baseline-preserving guard-dual diagnostic closes one of
eleven histories and also fails its admission threshold. The evidence supports
complete-obligation reductions, local mechanism effects, and an empirical map
of limitations, not stable superiority or a source-complete strict verifier.
Historical input-containment gaps and the numerical status of backend bounds
remain explicit. Larger-radius and single-layer boundary queues are pending.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}
Data-dependent dispatch makes a MoE a piecewise program. Proving that its route
never changes is sufficient in some cases but unnecessary for output robustness.
Conversely, finding an unsafe expert or a negative relaxation lower bound does
not establish an unsafe complete execution. The obligation concerns the same
input, the routers and experts it reaches, and every legal tie resolution.

Our question is which complete verification reductions expose that structure
effectively to existing engines, and when their additional cost is worthwhile.
We contribute a scoped organization of guarded obligations and a comparative
study of route decomposition, prefix pruning, and gate relaxation. The claim is
not that path splitting, weak duality, hybrid zonotopes, or endpoint affine
optimization is individually new. Whether their instantiation and the empirical
findings constitute a sufficient research contribution requires independent
technical and literature review.

The current experiments cover eval-mode CIFAR-10 MLPs with output or internal
selected-softmax top-2 routing, including residual variants. They use CPU/double
precision and an explicitly modified compatibility snapshot of the general
nonlinear verifier GenBaB~\cite{genbab}. They do not establish results for
arbitrary token-level MoE, native floating-point execution, or new high-accuracy
architectures. The earlier ACT implementation using HybridZ~\cite{hz} and its proof
artifacts supply comparison and trust-boundary evidence, not a replacement for
the stronger external engine.
In configuration names, $L$ is the number of routed layers, $E$ the experts per
layer, and $R$ denotes a residual variant. Model and training details remain
bound to the stage manifests rather than selected by verification success.

\section{Complete-output semantics and reductions}
For an input set $X$, let layer $\ell$ receive $h_{\ell-1}$, compute router
scores $r_{\ell,e}$ and expert outputs $E_{\ell,e}$, and select an unordered
pair $S_\ell=\{a,b\}$. The pair is legal whenever each selected score is at
least each unselected score. Equality is included. With a fixed pair,
\[
 h_\ell=\rho_\ell h_{\ell-1}
       +\lambda_\ell E_{\ell,a}(h_{\ell-1})
       +(1-\lambda_\ell)E_{\ell,b}(h_{\ell-1}),\qquad
 \lambda_\ell=\sigma(r_{\ell,a}-r_{\ell,b}),
\]
where $\rho_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ denotes a compatible residual connection. An
ordinary affine output head is allowed. For a history $H=(S_1,\ldots,S_L)$,
let $F_H$ be its specialized static graph and $g_{H,j}(x)\geq0$ all of its
prefix-dependent legality conditions. Define
$X_H=\{x\in X:\forall j,\ g_{H,j}(x)\geq0\}$.
The target is
\[
 \forall H\;\forall x\in X_H\;\forall k,\quad
 p_{H,k}(x)=q_k^\top F_H(x)+c_k>0.
\]
All quantities in a history use the same input and upstream selected execution.
A list of conservatively possible histories is not a proof that each is
reachable; observed route flips also do not exhaust the legal set.

\begin{proposition}[Complete-obligation composition]
Suppose a finite history cover contains every legal execution on $X$.
For every retained history and property, suppose a valid lower bound over
$X_H$ is strictly positive, or the history has a valid emptiness proof.
Then the complete MoE satisfies all the properties on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every legal execution belongs to a covered history and satisfies that history's
guards. Its output equals the specialized graph's output. Apply each valid
row bound. At a tie, the same argument applies to every legal choice.
\end{proof}
The hypotheses include valid input enclosure, correct graph conversion, and
sound route exclusions. A timeout, partially enumerated tree, or structural
receipt check does not supply a missing hypothesis. Domain-scoped facts may
replace an obligation only when their property and source match and their
proved domain contains the target guarded domain.

\begin{proposition}[Guarded specification as output atoms]
For a fixed history and property, the guarded property above is equivalent to
\[
 \forall x\in X,\quad
 s_H(x)=\max\{p_H(x),-g_{H,1}(x),\ldots,-g_{H,J}(x)\}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a legal point all $-g_{H,j}$ are nonpositive, so $s_H>0$ requires $p_H>0$.
At an illegal point at least one $-g_{H,j}$ is positive, satisfying the formula
regardless of the property. These cases exhaust $X$.
\end{proof}
The maximum is representable with ReLU operations. Strict positivity preserves
tie obligations; replacing it by a non-strict check would not prove the stated
property. Logical equivalence does not imply that a finite-budget relaxation
uses an output atom as effectively as an input-domain constraint. Prefix pruning
uses its own bounds to exclude impossible extensions, never another arm's
route answers. An unfinished extension remains in the coverage denominator.

\subsection{Independent gates and last-gate endpoints (M-A)}
For each history prefix, obtain a valid margin range and an enclosing sigmoid
range $[l_\ell,u_\ell]$. Replace its gate by an independent variable in that
range. Every original execution embeds in this enlarged relation by choosing
its actual gates. Inductively this gives an outer relaxation provided all
ranges are valid on the relevant original prefix domain and any retained
constraints also contain the original execution. Gate independence loses
router--expert correlation; it is not an exact transformation.

\begin{proposition}[Last-gate endpoint reduction]
Suppose the suffix after the last mixed expert output is affine, and the domain
of upstream variables $z$ and retained guards is independent of the last gate.
Write a property as $b(z)+\lambda d(z)$ for $\lambda\in[l,u]$. Then
\[
 \inf_{z,\lambda\in[l,u]}\{b(z)+\lambda d(z)\}
 =\min\{\inf_z[b(z)+l d(z)],\inf_z[b(z)+u d(z)]\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each $z$ the affine function of $\lambda$ attains its minimum at an
endpoint. Infimum commutes with the minimum of these two functions.
\end{proof}
Both endpoints must close for every property. This statement does not turn
all inner variable gates into pure ReLU computations; they still multiply
expert outputs. Nor does it justify endpoint substitution through a nonlinear
suffix. For fixed $z$, replacing a gate by another value within its range
changes this property by at most $(u-l)|d(z)|$. Multilayer output error requires
additional downstream sensitivity bounds, not a sum of unpropagated widths.

The complete-request cascade first tries the relaxed history with an at-most
120-second phase cap, then uses only the remaining request time for coupled
GenBaB. Every phase is charged to the same 300-second request. A failed relaxed
proof is UNKNOWN; a relaxed violating point requires original-model replay.
Keeping an old candidate does not recover time spent on a failed phase.

\subsection{Guard duals and margin partitions (M-B/M-C)}
For nonnegative multipliers $\beta$, any certified bound
\[
 L(\beta)\leq\inf_{x\in X}
       \left[p_H(x)-\sum_j\beta_jg_{H,j}(x)\right]
\]
is a lower bound for $p_H$ on $X_H$. This is weak duality, not a new theorem.
CROWN~\cite{crown} parameters and guard multipliers can be optimized jointly.
The zero multiplier permits an unconditioned candidate, but an independently
optimized finite trajectory need not reproduce that candidate's best bound.
The corrected diagnostic retains every baseline row maximum before refinement.
It separately pays for an equal-extra-alpha continuation to distinguish guard
benefit from additional optimization.

Even an exact scalar dual can have a gap. For $x\in[-1,1]$,
$p=x+1/10$ and $g=\operatorname{ReLU}(x)-1/4\geq0$, the constrained minimum
is $7/20$. The best scalar dual is instead
\[
 \sup_{\beta\geq0}\min\{-9/10+\beta/4,\;11/10-3\beta/4\}=-2/5.
\]
This analytic control explains why a negative dual bound alone cannot diagnose
an optimizer failure or unsafe original execution.

Closed margin cells can cover a parent domain, with overlaps at boundaries.
Each cell requires all output obligations; absent cells prevent a positive
aggregate. A cell restriction can be encoded as guard atoms or dualized rows,
with different finite relaxation behavior. The tested first-layer M-C used
output-side conditions. A proposed last-layer dual-conditioned variant was
conditional on revised M-B admission and was not run after that gate failed.

\section{Experimental design and evidence grades}
Training, checkpoint selection, input exclusion, radius, arms and stopping rules
are bound to stage-specific execution versions. Models use fixed validation
selection, not verification outcome selection. New verification inputs follow
a fixed ascending clean-correct rule outside historical identities; earlier
whole-test telemetry is disclosed. The primary cascade uses fifteen fixed
multilayer models (five configurations, three seeds) and twenty common inputs
at $2/255$. Three hundred model--input objects are not three hundred independent
images. Ten thousand bootstrap replicates resample the twenty input blocks,
carrying every model and paired arm together. Intervals describe these fixed
models, not random architectures or future training runs.

All arms use the same R7 compatibility engine within the paired comparison.
Compatibility changes, worker-tail repairs and failed attempts are disclosed;
the experiment is not an unchanged-author-code claim. One publication/cleanup
failure exceeded the outer limit, costing 301.579 seconds; it remains ERROR
and is charged. Later supervision reserves cleanup time, giving a 288-second
worker deadline within the 300-second request. Only unexecuted requests resume.
Complete costs include loading, propagation, queries, reception and cleanup.
Shared-host load limits fine-grained timing interpretations.

POSITIVE means a complete numerical filter under the frozen backend policy,
not outward-rounded formal SAFE. UNKNOWN, TIMEOUT and ERROR are kept distinct.
Public PGD counterexamples require full-model replay; they annotate the public
cohort and are not free arm-generated successes. Negative bounds are not
counterexamples. Structural audits recompute identities and accounting, not
all floating-point bounds. Independent rational LP checks and checked source
conversion are stronger but separate evidence grades.

\section{Results and stopped hypotheses}
\subsection{Routing decomposition and the multilayer bottleneck test}
A preceding selected single-layer comparison with guarded GenBaB passes all
60 historical positives and closes 17 of 41 historical failures; 22 remain
unknown and two are errors. This is a diagnostic cohort, not a new-input
population win. It motivates testing stronger generic engines directly.

The separate stage-1a study completes all 600 calls: three multilayer
configurations, three seeds, twenty inputs, three arms, plus sixty
single-layer pruning anchors. Table~\ref{tab:multilayer} shows 108/180
multilayer numerical positives for pruned GenBaB and none for naive enumeration
or ACT v1. ACT's zero consists of 178 TIMEOUT, one ERROR and one UNSUPPORTED,
not 180 solver timeouts. The single-layer anchor closes 52/60.

After excluding replayed unsafe cases, the L3E8 pruning arm leaves 19/52
requests without complete coverage and L2E16 leaves 11/58. These counts alone
do not identify combinatorial explosion. A supplemental attribution rule,
registered after a 189-call prefix, finds many-history dominance in only 2/52
and 0/58 respectively. That timing is disclosed: the rule is not retroactively
called fully preregistered. The route-union proposal stops under this rule.
The result rejects that finite proposal's motivation on these models, not all
multilayer routing bottlenecks or all HybridZ representations.

\subsection{Complete-request cascade: a real but insufficient gain}
Tables~\ref{tab:primary} and~\ref{tab:families} report the completed primary
comparison. Coupled and cascade arms share 113 positives; nine are gained and
three lost. The net $6/300=2$ percentage points has input-block 95\% interval
$[0,4]$. The registered admission requires a positive interval lower endpoint,
at least five percentage points, and median cost no more than twice baseline.
The practical-effect gate clearly fails; the decision is STOP, not gray.
Near-identical medians in a timeout-dominated table are not general speedup.

All nine gains use 26 relaxed history closures and no coupled fallback. Of
52 endpoints, 48 close in initial bound propagation and four in nonlinear
branch-and-bound. Gains concentrate in two-layer configurations. Their
1,530-second saving is outweighed by 2,459 extra seconds on 113 shared positives
and 334 on the three losses; neither-positive pairs save 155 seconds without
being solved. Net request cost rises by 1,109 seconds. Two losses spend large
budgets in unclosed relaxed phases. Another completes sixteen such phases but
runs out before covering all histories. These traces do not establish that a
different phase cap would fix the loss; the cap is not retuned.

The paid minus-M-A ablation on six fixed models yields 64 versus 68 positives,
six gains and two losses, with interval $[-0.833,8.333]$ points. Its median costs
are 35.894 versus 132.610 seconds. Separately paid coupled runs disagree on four
terminal outcomes, including one positive becoming TIMEOUT. Four outcome
changes among 120 are not an estimate of three-percentage-point SAFE noise;
several changes concern two nonpositive terminal categories.

Public PGD finds 26 replayed counterexamples with no positive conflict. Twelve
objects have multiple observed histories and show no gain or loss; one observed
history in the rest does not prove stability. The larger-radius and single-layer
queues are still running. Their outcomes will be reported separately and cannot
replace the primary cohort or stopping rule.

\subsection{Guard and margin diagnostics}
Original M-B compares two independent twenty-evaluation trajectories on eleven
histories and obtains no closure gain. That test is insufficient to isolate
adding guards after alpha optimization; its valid numerical records are kept.
Original M-C tests twenty-seven histories with first-margin two/four-cell
partitions. There are no added closures. Median complete initial-score changes
are $+0.149568$ and $+0.427032$ over 25 and 19 observed pairs respectively; two
and eight missing score pairs are not imputed. On the nineteen common complete
pairs, median initial-bound costs rise from 62.240 seconds to 123.406 and
207.825. These findings concern this implementation, not every conditional
margin split.

The corrected M-B test runs 22 paid calls on the same eleven histories, with a
shared twenty-evaluation alpha prefix and forty continuation updates. It
restores an actually evaluated alpha state, starts beta at zero, charges a
fixed small gradient probe, and retains all baseline row maxima. All calls
complete within budget; Table~\ref{tab:guard} gives one local closure versus
none for equal-extra-alpha. For that residual two-layer history, the minimum
bound moves from $-5.532507$ to $-5.268918$ with alpha alone and to $1.059180$
with guard refinement. All 99 baseline row bounds are retained; eleven new
positive rows produce only one fully closed history.

The raw median minimum-bound improvement over the original prefix is 57.960907,
but cannot all be assigned to guards. Against paid extra-alpha continuation,
eight objects improve and three decrease, with median difference 0.323164 in
unscaled model logit units. Different continuation trajectories may differ
without violating retention of their own prefix. All measured work, including
controls and saved-record recount, totals about 25.04 minutes. The unchanged
five-added-closures admission fails. M-B and the conditional last-margin M-C
follow-up close; no new B4 or B5 is started. This local numerical positive is
not a complete-request or source-complete certificate.

\section{Source contracts, history and independent evidence}
The historical output-layer ACT comparison gives 179 policy acceptances versus
156 for matched monolithic and 141 for the older strong monolithic path over
100 inputs and three models. Its 23 primary gains remain frozen experimental
outcomes. A later exact audit finds input-containment gaps affecting all 23;
no source-complete strict claim follows from those old bounds. The requested
and reconstructed input sets are not nested. This does not prove the trained
models unsafe or determine what corrected executions would produce.

A supplied LP can be checked independently with exact rational arithmetic.
For $Ax\leq b$, $l\leq x\leq u$, objective $c^\top x+c_0$, and $y\leq0$,
let $r=c-A^\top y$. A valid lower bound is
\[
 c_0+b^\top y+\sum_i\min\{r_i l_i,r_i u_i\}.
\]
Checking source coefficients, multiplier signs and the residual makes this a
statement about that LP. It does not establish neural-to-HZ conversion, original
input inclusion, guard lowering or omitted-route infeasibility. Portable old
requests check all required outputs conditional on their supplied source;
a newer checked source construction has not closed all new output obligations.
Old positive bounds are never attached to new matrices. No sealed request is
reopened for this paper.

\section{Related work and comparison limits}
Hybrid zonotopes~\cite{hz} provide mixed continuous/discrete representations;
our unfavorable ACT v1 result is not a verdict on the representation family.
CROWN~\cite{crown} and GenBaB~\cite{genbab} provide the underlying bound and
general-nonlinearity algorithms. Existing output-constraint work such as
INVPROP~\cite{invprop} and verification frameworks such as DNNV~\cite{dnnv}
preclude claiming generic constraint dualization or static-graph adaptation as
new. A routing-specific obligation contract and its evidence must carry the
contribution; adding a guard multiplier to a generic engine alone does not.

MetaMoE~\cite{metamoe} is a direct compositional-verification comparison with
a different, class-separated hard top-1 object. Our disclosed compatible
author-path comparison yields nine numerical positives versus four ACT policy
acceptances on ten inputs, with no ACT-only result. A separate earlier weighted
pair comparison gives eleven ACT policy acceptances versus thirteen plain-CROWN
numerical positives, with mean times 138.11 versus 4.23 seconds. Plain CROWN is
not the full GenBaB baseline used here. These cohorts, guarantees and costs are
not pooled.

Dual Randomized Smoothing~\cite{dualrs} certifies a smoothed variance selector
and classifier, including expert routing; its statistical $L_2$ guarantee is
not for our original deterministic function. RoME~\cite{rome} studies
multi-perturbation training with low-rank experts; Robust Experts~\cite{robustexperts}
studies intermediate CNN experts; J-TLAT~\cite{jtlat} jointly attacks and trains
video routers and experts. Feature Noise~\cite{featurenoise} studies robustness
under an equal-parameter, noisy-feature regime. These address related research
questions, but their attack accuracy or distributional risk is not our
per-input complete-output verification endpoint.

Our archived work includes a two-input Dual RS statistical pilot, two
Robust Experts training/evaluation variants, and a limited RoME attack batch.
It does not reproduce their entire paper tables or establish matched ACT
certification results. The J-TLAT implementation and a full Feature Noise
artifact were not obtained in the archived search; this is not a current claim
that no author artifact exists. The review guide links each statement to the
execution ledger and object-level semantic contracts. Continuous mixtures are
not silently replaced by top-2. Updated bibliographic versions do not relabel
the frozen implementations or their results. No six-paper common-task victory
or exhaustive novelty search is claimed.

\section{Threats, reproduction and disposition}
Selected hard-history diagnostics do not estimate full-request coverage.
Timeouts and frozen-gate failures identify finite execution outcomes, not unique
mathematical causes. In particular the evidence does not establish that all
remaining difficulty lies in expert ReLU networks. Multiple training seeds,
shared inputs, hardware variability, compatibility changes and reused observed
diagnostic objects constrain generalization. Nonlinear backend positive filters
are neither outward-rounded proofs nor deployed PyTorch/CUDA guarantees.

The four current tables are reconstructed without model or solver calls.
Run \path{scripts/render_current_review_tables.py} with \texttt{--check}.
They use committed
B4, multilayer-stage and revised-guard summaries. This checks accounting only.
The repository review guide maps claims to exact records and retained raw
identities. Historical table kits remain immutable historical snapshots; the
new manuscript does not silently update their manifests. Independent human
technical review, clean empirical execution, and distributable artifact rights

\section{Conclusion}
Complete guarded obligations let a general verifier analyze route-variable MoE
outputs without requiring route invariance. Prefix pruning and gate endpoints
can be useful, but this study's specialized cascade fails its registered
practical-effect gate, and the corrected guard diagnostic fails admission.
The defensible result is a collection of explicit reductions and measured
limits, including useful local effects, with source and numerical assumptions
visible. Completing the remaining fixed queues will refine the empirical map;
it will not retroactively turn failed gates into demonstrated superiority.
