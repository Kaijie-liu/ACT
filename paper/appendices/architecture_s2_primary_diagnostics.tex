\section{S2a primary measurements and their limits}
\label{app:s2widths}
The compact source export in \path{paper/results/s2a_primary.json} binds the
accepted primary summary, all twenty model identities and the per-seed
measurements. \path{scripts/architecture_s2_figure.py} checks complete batch
coverage, aggregates with input-count weights and renders the figure without
loading a checkpoint or evaluating a network. Its source-bound export permits
rerendering without access to server-only evidence paths. This is reproducible
analysis of accepted records, not independent recomputation of their bounds.

Each model averages over all 10,000 inputs; arm summaries then average five
seeds. An expert-layer aggregate gives every expert equal weight, since all
experts have the same layer widths. These measurements share the full input
box and therefore do not isolate guard-conditioned or active-path behavior.
Hidden-unit rescaling and different learned logit scales prevent interpreting
raw width ratios as architecture-invariant precision ratios. An unstable-ReLU
fraction is a bound diagnostic, not a count of necessary branch-and-bound
queries. There is no causal layer ablation in this study.

The complete primary request, including loading, batch evaluation, diagnostic
capture, serialization and acceptance, takes a mean of 14.543 seconds per
D-act model, 19.512 for D-tot, 21.099 for M and 21.014 for M$-$reg. Each request
contains all 10,000 images. These batched measurement costs must not be compared
as if they were the single-input complete-verifier costs in S1. Input
materialization and the later record/statistics analysis are charged separately;
the secondary tables report full single-input requests instead.

\section{S2a final evidence, seed counts and cost closure}
\label{app:s2final}
The final source-bound export \path{paper/results/s2a_final.json} retains all
complete/incomplete comparisons, paired gain/loss identities, per-model
terminal counts and costs. The 24,020 evaluation items comprise twenty
all-test primary batches, 20,000 incomplete requests and 4,000 complete
requests. These have different input denominators and must not be pooled
into a single certification rate.

One original incomplete D-act request failed cleanup and remains ERROR despite
a saved positive candidate. The dead-process parser was repaired without
changing numerical execution, and only previously unexecuted requests
continued in a separately bound executable. The final audit accounts for both
segments and verifies that the old failure was neither rerun nor upgraded.
All costs and terminal denominators reconcile. No clean-prediction difference
between the recorded float32 primary and float64 secondary executions, or
positive/replayed-violation conflict, was found on their common inputs. Absence
of such conflicts is not an independent proof of numerical soundness.

The request-time sums are 380.845 seconds for primary batches, 161,541.731
seconds for incomplete verification, and 209,148.569 seconds for complete
verification: 103.075 summed hours. The two active queue durations sum to
12.956 hours with at most eight single-thread CPU workers. This excludes an
approximately 561.6-second gap between segments, which contains the tool
repair and continuation preparation; it is not the start-to-finish elapsed
duration. Materialization, executable copying, admission, training, analysis
and manuscript preparation are recorded separately in the stage record.
The automatic final audit takes 44.235 seconds; a separate saved-record and
statistics recount takes 30.546 seconds, and repeating the earlier same-input
analysis takes 7.982 seconds including its subprocess wrapper. These checks
add no model, solver or witness-replay calls. They reproduce the primary,
incomplete and same-input reports without changing an online terminal.

The shared evaluation evidence account remains below its 128 MiB cap and
carries the original segment's bytes forward. Training weights are separate
from this evidence quota; the 24 training runs occupy 2,751,268,248 bytes of
model storage. No raw checkpoint or training data is included in the Git
exports. Record-level reproducibility is provided; a distributable empirical
artifact and independent human review still require PI-managed work.
