\begin{abstract}
Does conditional computation make a mixture of experts (MoE) easier to certify
than a dense network of matched capacity? In two pre-registered CIFAR-10 MLP
studies, we find no advantage meeting the stated accuracy--cost criteria.
Under ordinary training, complete verification obtains 114/600 numerical
positives for MoE versus 115/600 and 116/600 for total- and active-parameter
matched dense networks. Under certification training with five seeds and all
10,000 test images, MoE attains 30.12\% interval-bound-propagation (IBP)
certified accuracy, below 33.55\% and 31.97\% for the dense matches.
The deficit combines lower clean accuracy with a lower conditional
certification fraction against the active match. Smaller mean property-interval
widths do not reverse that deficit. A routing regularizer raises the sufficient
single-route fraction from 44.55\% to 73.92\%, but its primary certification
gain is only 0.20 percentage points, with a 95\% interval spanning zero.
To test the architecture question without assuming fixed routes, we give a
complete-output guarded reduction and a route-envelope IBP training objective
covering every tie-legal top-2 pair. The enclosure statements assume valid
constituent bounds; empirical positives follow a floating numerical policy.
These results delimit the benefits of sparsity and routing stability in the
registered setting, rather than ruling out advantages for other MoE families.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Sparse mixtures of experts activate a small part of their parameters at each
input. It is tempting to expect that the same narrow computation also makes
robustness cheaper to certify. This expectation overlooks two costs: the
verifier must cover every legal route in the perturbation domain, and each
selected expert must have enough predictive margin to withstand its bound
uncertainty. A small executed graph need not make a good certifiable classifier.

We test that expectation using total-parameter and active-parameter matching.
The first convention holds stored capacity approximately fixed; the second
holds the parameters used by one selected path approximately fixed, including
the router. Neither convention alone equates training objectives or optimization
difficulty. We retain ordinary and certification-trained models, all registered
seeds, and clean errors in the primary certification denominators.

Three claims organize the study. C1 is semantic: all legal executions can be
covered by guarded static verification obligations. C2 is empirical: ordinary
MoE training gives at least as much certification with lower complete-request
cost under either capacity convention. C3 is empirical: routing-aware
certification training improves the clean-accuracy, certified-accuracy, and
verification-cost tradeoff. We establish a conditional reduction for C1, but
neither C2 nor C3 meets its registered empirical criterion.

The reusable method is a route-envelope training bound: it projects each
output property into the experts, encloses all interval-compatible pairs, and
minimizes over the two endpoints of each retained gate range. It does not
assume that the clean route remains fixed. Its components---IBP, property
projection, case splitting, and endpoint minimization---are established ideas;
we do not claim a new abstract domain or priority over all MoE certification.
The empirical contribution is the paired test and its diagnostic limits.
Earlier attempts to improve the verifier are retained as a scoped negative
result, rather than omitted after changing the research direction.

\section{Route-aware complete-output verification}
At layer $\ell$, let $S_\ell=\{a,b\}$ be an unordered selected pair and let
$h_{\ell-1}$ be the output of its preceding execution. Define
\[
 m_\ell=r_{\ell,a}(h_{\ell-1})-r_{\ell,b}(h_{\ell-1}),\qquad
 h_\ell=\sigma(m_\ell)E_{\ell,a}(h_{\ell-1})+
        (1-\sigma(m_\ell))E_{\ell,b}(h_{\ell-1}).
\]
All selected scores must be at least all unselected scores. Ties admit every
legal pair. For a history $H=(S_1,\ldots,S_L)$, $F_H$ is the specialized graph
and $g_{H,j}(x)\geq0$ its prefix-dependent legality conditions. On input set
$X$, write $X_H=\{x\in X:g_{H,j}(x)\geq0\text{ for all }j\}$ and
$p_{H,k}(x)=q_k^\top F_H(x)+c_k$. We seek
\[
 \forall H\;\forall x\in X_H\;\forall k,\qquad p_{H,k}(x)>0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Complete history reduction]
If a finite history cover contains every legal execution on $X$, and each
history is proved infeasible or every output property has a positive valid
lower bound over $X_H$, the complete MoE satisfies all output properties on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any legal execution has a covered history, satisfies its guards, and agrees
with its specialized graph. Apply the lower bounds to that input. The argument
applies to every tie resolution, not just a library's selected clean route.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prefix exclusion]
Suppose a proposed next pair requires $r_a-r_c\geq0$. A valid strict negative
upper bound on $r_a-r_c$, over a domain containing every legal execution of the
preceding prefix, excludes this extension and all its descendants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The required inequality is impossible on that prefix. Any descendant requires
the same extension. A zero upper bound does not exclude equality.
\end{proof}
Our frontend computes its own prefix bounds; it does not borrow another arm's
route census. Numerical exclusions are recorded as such. A surviving candidate
is not a reachability witness, and an unfinished tree cannot establish safety.

\begin{proposition}[Guarded properties as output atoms]
For a fixed history and property,
\[
 \forall x\in X_H:p_H(x)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall x\in X:\max\{p_H(x),-g_{H,1}(x),\ldots,-g_{H,J}(x)\}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At legal points every negated guard is nonpositive, so the maximum is positive
exactly when the property is positive. At illegal points a negated guard is
strictly positive. This also explains why a non-strict acceptance test would
incorrectly dismiss a violated property at a tie.
\end{proof}
ReLU implements the maximum, permitting a standard nonlinear verifier such as
GenBaB~\cite{genbab}. Logical equivalence does not imply that an output-side
guard produces the same relaxation as constraining the input domain.

\begin{proposition}[Last-gate endpoints]
If the suffix is affine and the upstream domain $P$ and retained guards do not
depend on the last independent gate $\lambda\in[l,u]$, then
\[
 \inf_{z\in P,\,\lambda\in[l,u]}[b(z)+\lambda d(z)]
 =\min\{\inf_{z\in P}[b(z)+l d(z)],\inf_{z\in P}[b(z)+u d(z)]\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An affine function of $\lambda$ attains its minimum at an endpoint; infimum
commutes with the minimum of these two functions.
\end{proof}
Replacing gates by independently enclosing ranges is an outer relaxation,
not an equivalent network transformation. Every actual execution embeds by
using its actual gate values. Endpoints do not remove variable inner-gate
products or apply through a nonlinear suffix. This optional construction is
part of the prior study; the frozen S1 complete baseline uses coupled GenBaB.

\begin{lemma}[Why active parameters do not determine total cost]
For a fixed history only its selected expert graphs need be instantiated in
the specialized output, but its guards still require relevant router scores.
A complete request can incur
\[
 T=T_{\rm load}+T_{\rm route}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal H_{\rm visited}}
 (T_{\rm graph,H}+T_{\rm bound,H})+T_{\rm evidence/cleanup}.
\]
Thus bounding active parameters per history alone gives no bound independent
of the visited-history count or proof difficulty.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Static specialization removes unselected expert evaluations from that history;
it does not discharge other histories. Summing the work of the actually visited
obligations, including shared and non-shared overhead as implemented, gives the
decomposition. Arbitrarily many unresolved histories or expensive selected
networks remain consistent with the same per-history active parameter count.
\end{proof}
The sum describes work in one request. Parallel throughput across requests
must not be substituted for per-request cost. Our loader reads complete model
weights; that cost is charged even for weights absent from a specialized graph.

\section{Route-envelope IBP for certification training}
\label{sec:routeibp}
The S2a follow-up uses the complete-output semantics above to construct a
training bound for a single output MoE layer. It encloses every compatible
pair, including alternatives to the clean route. This section specifies the
implemented method; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports its failed primary improvement criterion.
The following statements concern real arithmetic and valid constituent
intervals. The implementation uses the numerical policy described in
Section~\ref{sec:numericalscope}.

\paragraph{Router cover and gate ranges.}
Let $X$ be the input box, and suppose IBP gives
$r_i(X)\subseteq[\ell_i,u_i]$. For a canonical pair $a<b$, retain
\[
 \mathcal C(X)=\bigl\{\{a,b\}:\quad
    u_a\geq\ell_c\ \text{and}\ u_b\geq\ell_c
    \text{ for every }c\notin\{a,b\}\bigr\}.
\]
For each retained pair, enclose the weight of its first expert by
\[
 [L_{ab},U_{ab}]=
 [\sigma(\ell_a-u_b),\sigma(u_a-\ell_b)].
\]
\begin{lemma}[Interval-compatible route cover]
Every tie-legal top-2 pair at every $x\in X$ belongs to $\mathcal C(X)$,
and its selected-softmax weight lies in $[L_{ab},U_{ab}]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Legality requires $r_a(x),r_b(x)\geq r_c(x)$ for every unselected $c$.
Consequently both selected upper bounds are at least $\ell_c$; a pair
failing the test cannot be legal. The margin lies between
$\ell_a-u_b$ and $u_a-\ell_b$; apply monotonicity of $\sigma$.
Equality is retained throughout.
\end{proof}
The test is exact for independent logit intervals: a retained pair is legal
at the score assignment $r_a=u_a,r_b=u_b,r_c=\ell_c$ for unselected $c$.
That assignment need not be attainable by the router on a common input, so
this is a cover rather than an exact reachability census.

\paragraph{Project before taking the expert envelope.}
For a property $q^\top F+c$, fold $q$ and $c$ into each expert's last affine
layer before interval propagation through that layer. Let the resulting valid
interval be $q^\top E_i(X)+c\subseteq[A_i,B_i]$. This retains cancellation
between output coordinates in the final affine expression; it does not
preserve all shared-input relations between experts. Define
\begin{align*}
 \underline p(X)&=\min_{\{a,b\}\in\mathcal C(X)}
       \min_{t\in\{L_{ab},U_{ab}\}}
       \bigl[tA_a+(1-t)A_b\bigr],\\
 \overline p(X)&=\max_{\{a,b\}\in\mathcal C(X)}
       \max_{t\in\{L_{ab},U_{ab}\}}
       \bigl[tB_a+(1-t)B_b\bigr].
\end{align*}
\begin{proposition}[Complete-output interval enclosure]
For every input in $X$ and every tie-legal selection,
$q^\top F(x)+c\in[\underline p(X),\overline p(X)]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The legal pair and its actual weight occur in the cover and gate range.
For any such weight $t\in[0,1]$, nonnegative mixing bounds the property
between $tA_a+(1-t)A_b$ and $tB_a+(1-t)B_b$; the constant is included once
because the weights sum to one. Each expression is affine in $t$, so its
extremum over the interval occurs at an endpoint. Taking the outer envelope
over all retained pairs completes the argument.
\end{proof}
Endpoint minimization is exact for this independent interval relaxation,
not for the original joint router--expert graph. This rule reuses the
last-gate principle; it is not a new abstract domain. \begin{proposition}[Dense degeneration]
For $E=1$ with the explicit top-1, weight-one convention, the route-envelope
bound equals property-projected dense IBP on that expert.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The cover contains the sole expert, its weight is one, and both gate
endpoints equal one. The envelope is precisely $[A_1,B_1]$.
Top-2 selection is not defined on a singleton set.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Training objective and stability diagnostic.}
For label $y$, apply the bound to all $K-1$ rows $q_j=e_y-e_j$, $c_j=0$,
obtaining $\underline p_j(X)$. The robust training term is
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm rob}(X,y)=
 \log\left(1+\sum_{j\ne y}\exp(-\underline p_j(X))\right).
\]
Every actual class margin is at least its lower bound, so monotonicity of
log-sum-exp makes this an upper bound on cross-entropy over all legal
executions, subject to the interval-validity assumptions. Training and primary
evaluation call the same bound function.
For the clean selected pair $S_0=\{a,b\}$, the exclusion penalty is
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm route}(X,S_0)=
 \sum_{c\notin S_0}
 [\tau-(\min(\ell_a,\ell_b)-u_c)]_+,\qquad\tau=0.
\]
Writing bars for batch means, the MoE loss is
$\kappa\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm CE}+(1-\kappa)\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm rob}
+\lambda_m\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm route}+0.1\mathcal L_{\rm balance}$;
the dense loss omits both routing terms. During natural warmup, $\kappa=1$
and the robust and exclusion terms are inactive. The balance term is retained
from the original MoE recipe, including during warmup, so the comparison
discloses this architecture-specific loss difference.
Zero exclusion penalty at $\tau=0$ permits ties and does not prove a unique
route. Instead, the reported sufficient single-route diagnostic is
$|\mathcal C(X)|=1$. A unique pair still allows input-dependent gate weights.
Large candidate covers and loose expert intervals can make the training
bound ineffective; neither the penalty nor this enclosure theorem guarantees
the accuracy or cost advantage in C3.


\section{Capacity-matched experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Models, data, and evaluation}
CIFAR-10 uses a fixed seed-0 45k/5k training/validation split and ToTensor only.
L1E8 has a 3072--128--8 router and eight 3072--256--128--10 experts, with top-2
selected softmax and no batch normalization. Its total and logical active
parameter counts are 6,961,368 and 2,036,124. Dense total matching uses widths
1758/879 and 6,957,295 parameters; dense active matching uses widths 602/301
and 2,034,469 parameters. Both mismatches are below 0.1\%. The router is
included in the MoE counts. Active parameters do not equal training FLOPs:
the implementation evaluates all experts before selection.

S1 uses three seeds, 50 epochs of ordinary training, AdamW at 0.001,
weight decay 0.0001, and batch size 256. MoE retains its original 0.1 balance
term; dense training uses cross-entropy. Earliest maximum validation accuracy
selects the checkpoint. L2E8 is a secondary depth extension, not another
capacity match. S2a uses five seeds and the same split, batch size, and weight
decay, with the route-envelope objective or dense IBP. Its 100 epochs comprise
five natural warmup epochs, a 40-epoch linear ramp to $2/255$ and
$\kappa=0.5$, then 55 epochs at those endpoints. Two seed-0 configurations
per arm are selected by validation IBP accuracy, then clean accuracy and
fixed tie breaks: learning rates $\{10^{-3},5\cdot10^{-4}\}$ for D-act,
D-tot, and M$-$reg; routing weights $\{0.1,1\}$ at learning rate $10^{-3}$
for M. Seed 0 is reused and the selected configuration trains seeds 1--4.
The selected D-act learning rate is $5\cdot10^{-4}$; the other arms use
$10^{-3}$ and M selects $\lambda_m=0.1$. No test result selects a model.

S2a's primary endpoint is IBP certified accuracy on all 10,000 test images
at $2/255$. Secondary CROWN-IBP uses the first 1,000 and complete verification
the first 200 inputs per model. S1 uses the same secondary prefix sizes at
$2/255$ and $8/255$. These deterministic prefixes have historical exposure;
they are not fresh holdouts. Complete verification uses the route reduction
and pruned GenBaB for MoE, and the corresponding dense graph for dense models.
S2a uses identical engine versions and nonlinear branching configuration for
both; S1 used native ReLU branching for dense models. Each full request has a
300-second budget including loading, routing, all property queries and result
acceptance. All-request cost includes every terminal state. Public PGD results
are separately computed and do not supply free answers to a verifier arm.
Numerical positives, full-model-replayed violations, returned unknowns,
timeouts, and errors are kept separate.

S1 resamples common input blocks with its three fixed seeds retained. S2a
uses 10,000 crossed paired seed/input bootstrap draws, resampling the five
seed IDs and common input IDs while keeping validation-selected configurations
fixed. Reported 95\% percentile intervals are unadjusted and do not quantify
uncertainty over architecture search. Model--input cells are not independent
images. The numerical interpretation is specified in Section~\ref{sec:numericalscope}.

\section{Mechanism diagnostics: accuracy, interval scale, and routing}
\label{sec:diagnostics}
\paragraph{An exact descriptive decomposition.}
Let $C$ be the clean-correct fraction, $R$ the positive fraction among those
correct predictions, and $A$ certified accuracy. By construction $A=CR$.
For M and D-act, the symmetric decomposition is
\[
 \Delta A=\Delta C\,\frac{R_M+R_D}{2}
          +\Delta R\,\frac{C_M+C_D}{2}.
\]
M's clean accuracy is 3.492 points lower, and its conditional positive fraction
is 2.365 points lower. The two arithmetic contributions to the $-3.430$-point
certification gap are $-2.274$ and $-1.156$ points, respectively.
Consequently the gap cannot be attributed entirely to clean accuracy.
Across four arms the conditional fractions range from 63.78\% to 66.31\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}). These are descriptive ratios on each arm's
own correct inputs; the decomposition is neither an intervention nor an
estimate of a causal training mechanism.

\paragraph{Narrower intervals are insufficient.}
The mean projected-property interval widths are 2.777 for D-act, 3.865 for
D-tot, 2.024 for M, and 2.385 for M$-$reg. Yet the narrower MoE intervals
coexist with lower certified accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:width}). Certification
requires every lower endpoint to be positive, which depends on interval
location as well as width. The learned logit scales also differ. Therefore
these numbers do not establish that one architecture has a tighter abstraction
of the same function, nor that its true clean logit margins are smaller.
The saved records contain interval bounds rather than all clean logit margins;
we do not substitute interval midpoints for those margins.

\paragraph{Path capacity and training exposure are plausible, unisolated factors.}
Each MoE expert has hidden widths 256/128, versus 602/301 for the active-matched
dense network. That difference may matter during certified training, but it
was not separately manipulated. Eight experts with top-2 routing implies an
average of two clean selections per input, not equal expert utilization or
exactly one quarter of the training data per expert. In particular the robust
loss computes intervals for all experts and envelopes all retained pairs;
its gradient exposure is not determined solely by the clean selected pair.
The balance term, optimization, and learned margins also differ. We therefore
report these as candidate explanations, not a uniquely established capacity
or data-allocation mechanism.

\paragraph{Routing regularization changes a diagnostic more than the primary outcome.}
The sufficient single-route rate rises from 44.55\% to 73.92\%, while the
primary certification contrast spans zero. Secondary CROWN-IBP shows a
larger positive regularizer effect. Thus the penalty affects more than
routing alone, but its routing improvement is insufficient for the registered
advantage. Likewise S1's 23-versus-2 incomplete result is a real, scoped
ordinary-training observation. Its disappearance as a dense-comparison
advantage under S2a does not retrospectively identify why the S1 bounds failed.

\section{Verifier evidence: routing was not the dominant measured bottleneck}
The earlier multilayer study obtains 108/180 numerical positives with pruned
GenBaB and 0/180 with ACT v1. The latter comprises 178 timeouts, one error,
and one unsupported case, not 180 solver timeouts. A supplemental attribution
rule finds many-history dominance in 2/52 non-replayed-unsafe L3E8 cases and
0/58 L2E16 cases. That rule was registered after a 189-call prefix; its timing
must not be described as fully pre-registered.

A gate-decoupling cascade gives 122/300 versus 116/300: nine gains, three
losses, net $+2$ points with input-block interval $[0,4]$. It misses the
registered five-point threshold. Its gains use decoupling, but its request
losses remain. Revised guard dualization adds only one local closure among
eleven histories, below its five-closure gate. The proposed last-layer
margin diagnostic was not executed after that stop. These records support
a limited bottleneck finding for the studied models and engine; they do not
prove that routing is always cheap or that expert ReLUs uniquely explain
all unresolved queries. Full cohort and outcome tables are in
Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}.

\section{Related work, guarantee scope, and limitations}
\label{sec:numericalscope}
Sparse-MoE robustness predates this study~\cite{robmoe,rter}.
MetaMoE composes guarantees for class-separated hard top-1 routing~\cite{metamoe};
our enclosure covers every tie-legal weighted top-2 output on an input box.
Dual randomized smoothing certifies a different smoothed classifier with
statistical $L_2$ guarantees~\cite{dualrs}; our task is deterministic
$L_\infty$ robustness. CertMoE studies route-aware smoothing for text~\cite{certmoe}.
These are semantic distinctions, not comparable certified-accuracy scores,
and they do not justify a blanket first-MoE-certification claim.

IBP and CROWN-IBP are established certified-training methods~\cite{ibp,crownibp}.
SABR uses selected small boxes~\cite{sabr}, TAPS combines IBP and adversarial
loss approximations~\cite{taps}, and expressive loss combinations further
study this tradeoff~\cite{mtlibp}. CTBench supplies implementation and benchmark
conventions~\cite{ctbench}. Our fixed IBP study neither reproduces nor
outperforms their stronger CNN training recipes.

The architecture-training conclusion is restricted to CIFAR-10 MLPs with
eight experts, top-2 routing, the registered tuning space, and $2/255$
certification training. S1 additionally evaluates $8/255$, and the historical
verifier appendix includes other depths and E=16, but neither extends the
S2a training claim. Convolutional training, larger training radii, smaller
expert counts, and SABR/TAPS comparisons remain untested future work. We do
not add such experiments after the failed gate. S1 has three seeds, S2a five;
only S2a primary certification covers the full test set. Loss differences and
historically exposed secondary prefixes limit causal and external conclusions.

The theorems assume valid real-valued source graphs and constituent bounds.
Experiments use floating IBP/CROWN/GenBaB numerical policies, without a proof
of outward-rounded network arithmetic or native PyTorch execution. Exact
checks of supplied LPs and structural record audits do not repair historical
input-enclosure or network-lowering gaps. We consistently use numerical
positive or numerical-policy certified accuracy for these results. The
new-environment subset reproduction is described in Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction};
it does not establish a stronger guarantee or independent human review.

\section{Conclusion}
Under both capacity conventions the ordinary-training screen fails, and
certification training does not recover the proposed advantage. More stable
routing and smaller mean property intervals are insufficient for better
whole-test certified accuracy in this setting. The guarded reduction and
route-envelope objective remain reusable ways to evaluate variable-route
MoE without dropping legal executions. The negative findings delimit these
specific architecture and training arguments; they are not a theorem that
MoE can never be easier to certify.

\paragraph{Potential impact.}
Complete-route accounting can prevent optimistic robustness claims based on
one clean route. Conversely, numerical positives can be mistaken for
machine-checked deployment guarantees; this manuscript separates those
levels and does not recommend safety-critical deployment from these results.
