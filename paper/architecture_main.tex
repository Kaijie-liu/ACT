\begin{abstract}
Conditional computation reduces the number of expert parameters used by a
single MoE execution. Certification must cover every legal execution on a
perturbation domain. We give a guarded reduction for selected-softmax top-2
networks and an interval training objective covering all tie-legal routes.
We evaluate the architecture question under total- and active-parameter
matching on CIFAR-10 MLPs. An ordinary-training study finds 114/600 complete
numerical positives for MoE against 115/600 and 116/600 for the dense matches;
its registered screen fails. CROWN-IBP provides a smaller coverage advantage,
23/3000 versus 2/3000, with higher median cost. A subsequent certification-training
study uses five seeds and the full 10,000-image test set. Regularized MoE
attains 30.12\% IBP certified accuracy, below active-matched dense training
at 33.55\% and total-matched training at 31.97\%. The routing penalty raises
the single-route fraction from 44.55\% to 73.92\%, while its certification
gain is 0.20 percentage points with an interval spanning zero. Both studies
therefore stop under their registered rules. These results identify limits
of active-capacity and route-stability arguments in this fixed setting.
Secondary verification of the trained models remains in progress. Experimental
positives use a numerical policy; the mathematical claims state their source
and bound-validity assumptions explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and hypotheses}
A sparse MoE can represent many expert functions while activating only a few
at an input. Certification asks a different question: can every legal execution
on a neighborhood be shown to preserve the output property? The routers,
alternative histories, and their construction costs cannot be ignored.
We distinguish three claims and their required evidence.
\begin{itemize}
\item C1: guarded static verification obligations cover the route-variable
network. The reduction below establishes this statement under explicit source
and bound-validity assumptions. Small routing overhead is an empirical
condition, not part of the theorem.
\item C2: at matched capacity, MoE obtains at least as much certification with
lower complete-request cost. Total and active parameter matching answer
different questions; both must be reported.
\item C3: a routing-aware training objective improves the joint tradeoff among
clean accuracy, certified accuracy, and verification cost relative to dense
certification training. This is a gated research hypothesis, not a consequence
of a routing-margin penalty alone.
\end{itemize}
The contribution combines explicit routing semantics, a full-route training
bound, and tests that separate capacity, accuracy, routing and cost. Hybrid
zonotopes, case splitting, interval training, weak duality and endpoint
minimization are established components.
Precedents already study sparse-MoE robustness~\cite{robmoe}, compositional
MoE verification~\cite{metamoe}, and certification with routed smoothed
experts~\cite{dualrs}. The new empirical question needs a shared deterministic
function, threat model, capacity convention, and full cost denominator.

\section{Route-aware complete-output verification}
At layer $\ell$, let $S_\ell=\{a,b\}$ be an unordered selected pair and let
$h_{\ell-1}$ be the output of its preceding execution. Define
\[
 m_\ell=r_{\ell,a}(h_{\ell-1})-r_{\ell,b}(h_{\ell-1}),\qquad
 h_\ell=\sigma(m_\ell)E_{\ell,a}(h_{\ell-1})+
        (1-\sigma(m_\ell))E_{\ell,b}(h_{\ell-1}).
\]
All selected scores must be at least all unselected scores. Ties admit every
legal pair. For a history $H=(S_1,\ldots,S_L)$, $F_H$ is the specialized graph
and $g_{H,j}(x)\geq0$ its prefix-dependent legality conditions. On input set
$X$, write $X_H=\{x\in X:g_{H,j}(x)\geq0\text{ for all }j\}$ and
$p_{H,k}(x)=q_k^\top F_H(x)+c_k$. We seek
\[
 \forall H\;\forall x\in X_H\;\forall k,\qquad p_{H,k}(x)>0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Complete history reduction]
If a finite history cover contains every legal execution on $X$, and each
history is proved infeasible or every output property has a positive valid
lower bound over $X_H$, the complete MoE satisfies all output properties on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any legal execution has a covered history, satisfies its guards, and agrees
with its specialized graph. Apply the lower bounds to that input. The argument
applies to every tie resolution, not just a library's selected clean route.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prefix exclusion]
Suppose a proposed next pair requires $r_a-r_c\geq0$. A valid strict negative
upper bound on $r_a-r_c$, over a domain containing every legal execution of the
preceding prefix, excludes this extension and all its descendants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The required inequality is impossible on that prefix. Any descendant requires
the same extension. A zero upper bound does not exclude equality.
\end{proof}
Our frontend computes its own prefix bounds; it does not borrow another arm's
route census. Numerical exclusions are recorded as such. A surviving candidate
is not a reachability witness, and an unfinished tree cannot establish safety.

\begin{proposition}[Guarded properties as output atoms]
For a fixed history and property,
\[
 \forall x\in X_H:p_H(x)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall x\in X:\max\{p_H(x),-g_{H,1}(x),\ldots,-g_{H,J}(x)\}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At legal points every negated guard is nonpositive, so the maximum is positive
exactly when the property is positive. At illegal points a negated guard is
strictly positive. This also explains why a non-strict acceptance test would
incorrectly dismiss a violated property at a tie.
\end{proof}
ReLU implements the maximum, permitting a standard nonlinear verifier such as
GenBaB~\cite{genbab}. Logical equivalence does not imply that an output-side
guard produces the same relaxation as constraining the input domain.

\begin{proposition}[Last-gate endpoints]
If the suffix is affine and the upstream domain $P$ and retained guards do not
depend on the last independent gate $\lambda\in[l,u]$, then
\[
 \inf_{z\in P,\,\lambda\in[l,u]}[b(z)+\lambda d(z)]
 =\min\{\inf_{z\in P}[b(z)+l d(z)],\inf_{z\in P}[b(z)+u d(z)]\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An affine function of $\lambda$ attains its minimum at an endpoint; infimum
commutes with the minimum of these two functions.
\end{proof}
Replacing gates by independently enclosing ranges is an outer relaxation,
not an equivalent network transformation. Every actual execution embeds by
using its actual gate values. Endpoints do not remove variable inner-gate
products or apply through a nonlinear suffix. This optional construction is
part of the prior study; the frozen S1 complete baseline uses coupled GenBaB.

\begin{lemma}[Why active parameters do not determine total cost]
For a fixed history only its selected expert graphs need be instantiated in
the specialized output, but its guards still require relevant router scores.
A complete request can incur
\[
 T=T_{\rm load}+T_{\rm route}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal H_{\rm visited}}
 (T_{\rm graph,H}+T_{\rm bound,H})+T_{\rm evidence/cleanup}.
\]
Thus bounding active parameters per history alone gives no bound independent
of the visited-history count or proof difficulty.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Static specialization removes unselected expert evaluations from that history;
it does not discharge other histories. Summing the work of the actually visited
obligations, including shared and non-shared overhead as implemented, gives the
decomposition. Arbitrarily many unresolved histories or expensive selected
networks remain consistent with the same per-history active parameter count.
\end{proof}
The sum describes work in one request. Parallel throughput across requests
must not be substituted for per-request cost. Our loader reads complete model
weights; that cost is charged even for weights absent from a specialized graph.

\section{Route-envelope IBP for certification training}
\label{sec:routeibp}
The S2a follow-up uses the complete-output semantics above to construct a
training bound for a single output MoE layer. It encloses every compatible
pair, including alternatives to the clean route. This section specifies the
implemented method; Section 4 reports its failed primary improvement criterion.
The following statements concern real arithmetic and valid constituent
intervals. The implementation uses the numerical policy described in
Section~\ref{sec:numericalscope}.

\paragraph{Router cover and gate ranges.}
Let $X$ be the input box, and suppose IBP gives
$r_i(X)\subseteq[\ell_i,u_i]$. For a canonical pair $a<b$, retain
\[
 \mathcal C(X)=\bigl\{\{a,b\}:\quad
    u_a\geq\ell_c\ \text{and}\ u_b\geq\ell_c
    \text{ for every }c\notin\{a,b\}\bigr\}.
\]
For each retained pair, enclose the weight of its first expert by
\[
 [L_{ab},U_{ab}]=
 [\sigma(\ell_a-u_b),\sigma(u_a-\ell_b)].
\]
\begin{lemma}[Interval-compatible route cover]
Every tie-legal top-2 pair at every $x\in X$ belongs to $\mathcal C(X)$,
and its selected-softmax weight lies in $[L_{ab},U_{ab}]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Legality requires $r_a(x),r_b(x)\geq r_c(x)$ for every unselected $c$.
Consequently both selected upper bounds are at least $\ell_c$; a pair
failing the test cannot be legal. The margin lies between
$\ell_a-u_b$ and $u_a-\ell_b$; apply monotonicity of $\sigma$.
Equality is retained throughout.
\end{proof}
The test is exact for independent logit intervals: a retained pair is legal
at the score assignment $r_a=u_a,r_b=u_b,r_c=\ell_c$ for unselected $c$.
That assignment need not be attainable by the router on a common input, so
this is a cover rather than an exact reachability census.

\paragraph{Project before taking the expert envelope.}
For a property $q^\top F+c$, fold $q$ and $c$ into each expert's last affine
layer before interval propagation through that layer. Let the resulting valid
interval be $q^\top E_i(X)+c\subseteq[A_i,B_i]$. This retains cancellation
between output coordinates in the final affine expression; it does not
preserve all shared-input relations between experts. Define
\begin{align*}
 \underline p(X)&=\min_{\{a,b\}\in\mathcal C(X)}
       \min_{t\in\{L_{ab},U_{ab}\}}
       \bigl[tA_a+(1-t)A_b\bigr],\\
 \overline p(X)&=\max_{\{a,b\}\in\mathcal C(X)}
       \max_{t\in\{L_{ab},U_{ab}\}}
       \bigl[tB_a+(1-t)B_b\bigr].
\end{align*}
\begin{proposition}[Complete-output interval enclosure]
For every input in $X$ and every tie-legal selection,
$q^\top F(x)+c\in[\underline p(X),\overline p(X)]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The legal pair and its actual weight occur in the cover and gate range.
For any such weight $t\in[0,1]$, nonnegative mixing bounds the property
between $tA_a+(1-t)A_b$ and $tB_a+(1-t)B_b$; the constant is included once
because the weights sum to one. Each expression is affine in $t$, so its
extremum over the interval occurs at an endpoint. Taking the outer envelope
over all retained pairs completes the argument.
\end{proof}
Endpoint minimization is exact for this independent interval relaxation,
not for the original joint router--expert graph. This rule reuses the
last-gate principle; it is not a new abstract domain. A singleton expert set
has the explicit top-1, weight-one degeneration to dense IBP, rather than
an invalid top-2 selection from one expert.

\paragraph{Training objective and stability diagnostic.}
For label $y$, apply the bound to all $K-1$ rows $q_j=e_y-e_j$, $c_j=0$,
obtaining $\underline p_j(X)$. The robust training term is
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm rob}(X,y)=
 \log\left(1+\sum_{j\ne y}\exp(-\underline p_j(X))\right).
\]
Every actual class margin is at least its lower bound, so monotonicity of
log-sum-exp makes this an upper bound on cross-entropy over all legal
executions, subject to the interval-validity assumptions. Training and primary
evaluation call the same bound function.
For the clean selected pair $S_0=\{a,b\}$, the exclusion penalty is
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm route}(X,S_0)=
 \sum_{c\notin S_0}
 [\tau-(\min(\ell_a,\ell_b)-u_c)]_+,\qquad\tau=0.
\]
Writing bars for batch means, the MoE loss is
$\kappa\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm CE}+(1-\kappa)\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm rob}
+\lambda_m\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm route}+0.1\mathcal L_{\rm balance}$;
the dense loss omits both routing terms. During natural warmup, $\kappa=1$
and the robust and exclusion terms are inactive. The balance term is retained
from the original MoE recipe, including during warmup, so the comparison
discloses this architecture-specific loss difference.
Zero exclusion penalty at $\tau=0$ permits ties and does not prove a unique
route. Instead, the reported sufficient single-route diagnostic is
$|\mathcal C(X)|=1$. A unique pair still allows input-dependent gate weights.
Large candidate covers and loose expert intervals can make the training
bound ineffective; neither the penalty nor this enclosure theorem guarantees
the accuracy or cost advantage in C3.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{S1: frozen architecture screen}
CIFAR-10 uses a shared seed-0 45k/5k training/validation split, ToTensor only,
50 epochs, AdamW at 0.001 with weight decay 0.0001 and batch 256. Three seeds are
retained for each model. Earliest maximal validation accuracy selects the
checkpoint; test accuracy does not select it. Existing L1E8/L2E8 models use
cross-entropy plus their original 0.1 routing-balance term; dense controls use
cross-entropy. This architecture-specific objective difference is disclosed,
not presented as a pure architecture intervention with identical losses.

L1E8 has a 3072--128--8 router and eight 3072--256--128--10 experts. Router
parameters count in both capacity conventions. The dense width ratio 2:1 and
nearest integer parameter count are fixed before outcomes, without dummy
weights.

relaxation bounds do not prove safety and unsafety, respectively.

The primary endpoint is the 2/255 complete positive rate and all-request median

at least dense and a lower median time. Input-block bootstrap uses 10000
resamples with all three seed pairs retained. Intervals are descriptive here,
not an additional gate. A literal zero/zero rate comparison can pass this
screen on time; such a pass authorizes S2 but does not establish C2. All states,
missing costs and shared-clean-correct explanatory strata are reported.
Neither L2 nor 8/255 rescues a failed primary comparison.

\subsection{Incomplete bounds: a coverage--cost tradeoff}

\subsection{S2a: certification training on the full test set}
S1 stops under its registered rule. Evaluating ordinary-trained networks using
CROWN-IBP does not test certification training or the C3 tradeoff claim.
The PI subsequently authorized a separate S2a study to test C3 directly;
this decision does not convert S1 into a passed screen.
The frozen follow-up compares route-envelope IBP training, its routing-regularizer
ablation, and two capacity-matched dense IBP baselines across five seeds.
All 10,000 test inputs form its primary IBP endpoint; complete verification is
secondary and uses a common engine version and branching configuration.
It resamples paired seeds and inputs, while keeping validation-selected
configurations fixed. The bound and loss are given in
Section~\ref{sec:routeibp}. Training uses five natural warmup epochs, a
40-epoch linear ramp to $\epsilon=2/255$ and $\kappa=0.5$, then 55 epochs
at those endpoints. All arms use the same split, batch size and AdamW weight
decay as S1, with constant learning rate. Each arm has two seed-0 configurations:
learning rate $\{10^{-3},5\cdot10^{-4}\}$ for both dense arms and the
unregularized MoE; $\lambda_m\in\{0.1,1\}$ at learning rate $10^{-3}$
for the regularized MoE. Validation IBP accuracy selects the configuration and
checkpoint, with clean accuracy and fixed tie-breaks; the selected seed-0
model is reused and its configuration trains seeds 1--4. This gives 24 runs
and 20 evaluation models without test-driven selection.
The registered gate requires MoE minus active-matched dense IBP accuracy of
at least two percentage points with a strictly positive 95\% lower limit,
a nonnegative point difference against total matching, and a single-route
fraction of at least 90\%. All three must hold on the full 10,000-input
denominator; neither clean-correct filtering nor a secondary verifier result
rescues a failed gate. All 24 training runs completed, and all 20 primary
evaluations were accepted within their online budgets. The selected learning
rate is $5\cdot10^{-4}$ for D-act and $10^{-3}$ for the other arms; M selects
$\lambda_m=0.1$. These choices use validation data only.

95\% interval $[-4.108,-2.762]$; M minus D-tot is $-1.848$ points,
interval $[-2.632,-1.130]$. Each seed also has fewer M positives than either
dense match. The 50,000 model--input cells per arm are not 50,000 independent
images. The bootstrap resamples five seed IDs and 10,000 common input IDs,
keeping the selected configuration fixed; it does not quantify uncertainty
over architecture or hyperparameter search.

The regularizer changes routing more clearly than certification. M minus
M$-$reg raises the mean single-route fraction by 29.370 points, but the
IBP accuracy difference is only $+0.200$ points, interval $[-0.430,0.790]$.
The regularizer's zero loss permits ties, and the reported single-route
condition is the sufficient candidate-cover test from Section~\ref{sec:routeibp}.
Improving that condition does not ensure a favorable accuracy--certification
tradeoff. Conditioning on clean correctness does not rescue the comparison:
M's descriptive positive fraction is 63.95\%, versus 66.31\% for D-act and
63.82\% for D-tot, on different clean-correct cohorts.

Saved layer measurements provide a limited check of the narrow-path explanation.
Mean final-property interval widths are 2.777 for D-act, 3.865 for D-tot,
2.024 for M and 2.385 for M$-$reg. A smaller width alone does not determine
whether a true-minus-other margin is positive; the learned centers and output
scales also differ. Appendix~\ref{app:s2widths} reports hidden-layer widths
and unstable fractions. These are descriptive observations of the S2a models,
not a causal explanation of S1's CROWN-IBP result or a localization of every
unresolved proof.

The primary decision is \texttt{STOP\_S2A\_WRITE\_RESULTS}. Training required
14.282 hours of wall time and 50.846 summed task hours. The fixed 20,000
CROWN-IBP and 4,000 complete requests continue for the secondary report with
the same engine revision and branching settings for both families. Their
costs and final audit are pending. No convolutional extension, new sample,
new configuration or new verifier mechanism is launched after this stop.

\subsection{Earlier verifier experiments as motivation}
The archived multilayer study obtains 108/180 numerical positives with pruned
GenBaB and none with ACT v1. A subsequent gate-decoupling cascade obtains 122/300
against 116/300: nine gains, three losses, net 2 percentage points, input-block
interval [0,4]. It fails its five-point threshold. The corrected guard diagnostic
closes one of eleven histories and fails admission. These are retained negative
results, not evidence that dense architectures are harder to certify or that
expert ReLUs uniquely explain every unresolved query. The cohorts were selected
differently from S1 and cannot be pooled into its accuracy denominator.
Appendix~\ref{app:empirical} incorporates the archived model and outcome tables,
their selection boundaries, and the stopped mechanisms; these are not new runs.

\section{Related work and limits}
\label{sec:numericalscope}
Sparse-MoE robustness and dense compute comparisons precede this study~\cite{robmoe}.
MetaMoE uses class-separated hard top-1 compositional verification~\cite{metamoe}.
Dual randomized smoothing certifies a different, statistical $L_2$ function
with routed experts~\cite{dualrs}. RT-ER/JTDMoE already motivates expert-focused
robust training~\cite{rter}. CertMoE is recent route-aware text certification
work~\cite{certmoe}. None of these differences warrants a blanket ``first MoE
certification'' claim, and attack accuracies or smoothed-function guarantees
cannot substitute for our deterministic $L_\infty$ endpoint. The five-work source
review is available in the project record; author claims are not independent
proofs by this study.

Certified training already offers richer loss and training-region choices.
SABR uses small selected boxes to approximate the worst-case loss~\cite{sabr};
TAPS combines IBP and PGD loss approximations~\cite{taps}; expressive loss
combinations study the tradeoff between such objectives~\cite{mtlibp}.
CTBench provides implementations and benchmarking conventions~\cite{ctbench}.
We use a fixed common IBP schedule to test the proposed route-envelope method,
without claiming to reproduce or outperform these authors' CNN recipes.

The experiments cover small MLPs: three ordinary-training seeds in S1 and
five certification-training seeds in S2a. Parameter counts are approximately
matched, S1 and secondary S2a prefixes have historical exposure, and MoE
retains its architecture-specific balance term. S2a's primary endpoint covers
the entire test set, but its finite tuning space and one fixed training schedule
limit generalization. Convolutional models and stronger certified-training
recipes were conditional on a gate that failed and have not been evaluated.
All experimental backend positives use a floating numerical policy. Exact
arithmetic on a supplied LP cannot repair an unverified input enclosure or
network conversion. Historical source gaps remain, and the new study does
not claim a source-complete proof of native PyTorch execution. The mathematical
reductions assume valid sources and bounds. A successful record audit checks
identity and coverage, not every numerical bound. Human technical review and
clean-environment reproduction remain outstanding.

\section{Disposition}
S1 fails its architecture screen under both capacity conventions. S2a's
completed primary endpoint also fails: neither more stable routing nor smaller
mean interval widths yields the required trained certification advantage.
C2 and C3 are unsupported in these registered settings. C1 supplies a
conditional semantic basis for comparing full-output verification, while the
experiments delimit what follows from sparsity and routing statistics.
The remaining frozen secondary evaluation will complete the empirical record;
it will not revise either primary stop. Independent human technical review
and clean-environment empirical reproduction remain required before submission.
