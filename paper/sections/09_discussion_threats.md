# Discussion and threats to validity

## What the evidence establishes

The historical confirmation experiment shows a reproducible gain over two registered
internal monolithic configurations on new verification inputs. It does not
establish that smaller per-branch problems always solve faster: the preceding
four-arm experiment favored monolithic coverage, and its results remain part
of the record. The new scheduler is an empirical organization of proof work,
not an optimal algorithm-selection theorem. Its 25% allocation is frozen rather
than chosen anew for each reported test result.

The relationship ablation addresses a different question from scheduling.
Replacing shared factors by independent expert products is a sound enlargement
of the joint reachable relation. It can lose a proof without making the real
network unsafe. Two multi-route gains face completed-relaxation failures in
that control, supporting a precision mechanism. The third gain faces a solver
limit, and the product also changes problem size. These observations do not
identify every runtime or coverage difference as a pure correlation effect.

The stronger external studies constrain what those results can imply.
The registered historical-success cohort has 60/60 GenBaB numerical positives,
with no observed ACT-only request. Its success-based selection, 43 distinct
inputs and older ACT comparison version prevent a population or paired-speedup
claim. The separate 41-request failure-selected cohort has 17 external positives,
22 UNKNOWN and two ERROR. Neither selected cohort replaces a new-input,
contemporaneous complete-request comparison. Evidence:
[success cohort](../../docs/advantage_region_results_20261021.md),
[failure cohort](../../docs/b2_observed_results_20261021.md).

The completed multilayer comparison gives pruned GenBaB 108/180 positives and
ACT v1 zero, with the latter's 178 TIMEOUT, one ERROR and one UNSUPPORTED all
retained. The post-prefix many-history criterion stopped the proposed union
mechanism. Its timing-based categories are operational labels, not a unique
causal separation of network difficulty, guard precision and execution overhead.
The post-189 registration must remain visible; it must not be described as
pre-registered before all outcomes. Evidence:
[multilayer result](../../docs/multilayer_stage1_20261022.md).

The original M-B and M-C implementations subsequently fail their finite diagnostic admission rules. A higher
initial score on a subset of completed M-C tables is not a complete-history
gain; missing tables and the extra analysis cost remain part of the result.
This does not refute weak duality or all possible margin partitions. It does
remove empirical support for the proposed three-component advantage in the
tested implementations. M-A-only B4 now has a completed primary comparison:
122 versus 116 numerical positives, nine gains and three losses, and a net
two-point increase with an input-block interval of [0,4] points. The registered
five-point practical threshold fails. This is not a reason to expand the sample
or tune the phase allowance. The radius and single-layer queues remain separate
pending characterization. See the [diagnostic table](08_evaluation.md#multilayer-mechanism-diagnostics-no-admitted-m-b-or-m-c-benefit)
and [primary result](08_evaluation.md#complete-request-cascade-primary-comparison-practical-gate-not-met).

The original M-B test confounds guard use with two independent optimization
trajectories. Its revised baseline-preserving test remedies that comparison
and includes a paid extra-alpha continuation. It adds one local history
closure, showing that the old negative result did not establish the absence
of guard value. Nevertheless, one falls short of the unchanged five-closure
admission requirement; the conditional last-layer slicing study is not run.
Even exact multiplier optimization need not remove a nonlinear guard's
Lagrangian gap, as the analytic example in the method shows. The observed
residual bounds therefore do not identify ReLU difficulty, gate decoupling
or incomplete optimization as a unique cause.

The saved traces distinguish local usefulness from overall cost. All nine gains
use 26 decoupled history closures without coupled fallback, with 48 of 52
endpoints closed by initial bounds. However, the nine gains save about 1,530
seconds while the 113 shared-positive objects add about 2,459 seconds. Two losses
spend much of their budget in unfinished decoupled phases; another loses after
16 positive phases because repeated endpoint and phase costs exhaust the request
budget. These observations explain recorded execution patterns, not a guarantee
that another phase cap would recover the losses or that the relaxation optimum
is nonpositive. On the six-model ablation subset, separately paid coupled runs
also differ on four terminal statuses, including one POSITIVE/TIMEOUT change.
Finite-budget execution variability remains visible instead of being attributed
entirely to the mechanism.

## Model and input generality

The confirmed checkpoints are three training runs of one MLP family, with clean
accuracy around 48%. Their seeds also affect training splits. They do not
constitute an initialization-only causal experiment or independent evidence
across architectures. Common clean-correct selection and a fixed 2/255 radius
make paired tasks comparable but limit population interpretation. A 100-input
cluster bootstrap preserves the three models within an input; it cannot justify
inference over an unobserved population of model families. Previous whole-test
telemetry means these are new verification endpoints, not necessarily images
never inspected in any form. The earlier 30-input study is not pooled into the
100-input confirmation, and the historical 2/3 composite result is not relabeled.

The higher-accuracy AdvMoE checkpoint and its failed strict pilot are not a
substitute for positive high-accuracy certificates. A second, convolutional
family has now been evaluated with a separately fixed architecture, training
recipe, checkpoint selection and comparison protocol. Its 30-input comparison
produces no HZ-policy SAFE, and its 20-input general evidence study produces no
complete positive proof. These are explicit limits on cross-family transfer,
not pending experiments or a reason to select easier replacement models.

## Baselines, costs and fairness

The matched and legacy monolithic paths are ACT-internal comparisons sharing
much of the representation and numerical machinery. The external path uses
ACT route coverage and plain CROWN on whole-box static weighted pairs; it is
neither an independent verifier for the raw dynamic model nor full
alpha-beta-CROWN with branch-and-bound. A whole-box static proof is a valid
sufficient condition, even though it omits pair-guard conditioning. Its failure
cannot establish a violation of the guarded model. Its lower cost and larger
positive set in the observed cohort must nevertheless be reported directly.

The repaired MetaMoE author-checkpoint comparison adds a direct adverse case:
four ACT policy acceptances versus nine author numerical sufficient filters,
four shared and zero ACT-only. All ACT positives are route-stable. The repair
only turns the failing author request into a timeout; it produces no new
certificate. ACT's smaller all-request mean is driven by that unresolved
timeout, while its four common-positive requests are slower. Different
guarantee grades require separate labels, not dismissal of this competition.
The adapter and compatibility modification must be disclosed; the result
neither measures unchanged author execution nor implies universal superiority.

Observed average cost includes unsuccessful capped requests; lower cost is not
synonymous with faster solving of the same outcome. Cross-environment import
and handoff costs are included in the external request budget. Shared raw-input
materialization is disclosed separately. Counterexample counts are not an
attack-strength competition: the external path uses fixed conformance probes,
whereas ACT may discover candidates during optimization. Every accepted
UNSAFE still requires full-model replay. Post-selected ACT-only proof cases
are mechanism checks, not an unbiased estimate of independent proof success.

## Numerical and semantic trust

The main-table applicability audit makes an upstream limitation concrete for
the first family too. Exact checking of saved boxes fails requested-real-box
containment on all 100 inputs; the frozen midpoint/radius reconstruction also
fails materialized-box containment on 98 inputs, including all 23 gains.
This is not proof of model unsafety or recovered historical intermediate
states. However, hash consistency, small error magnitude and a common defect
across arms do not close the missing guarantee. The main performance table
must be read as comparative policy-accepted outcomes rather than independently
proved real-network certificates. No old counts are replaced and no old
positive bounds are attached to newly repaired source matrices. Evidence:
[main-table source applicability](../../docs/main_table_source_applicability_20260921.md).
The composed-input check directly shows nonnested requested and reconstructed
boxes on all 100 inputs, under both rational and binary64 radius semantics.
An input-only repair cannot upgrade the old bounds: omitted inputs may change
route obligations, and downstream conversion and numerical bounds still need
their own evidence on the same new object. The relative gains after such a
repair are unknown. Nor does the absence of a strict certificate imply an
unsafe model.

All tie-legal top-k sets are obligations in the registered real-valued model.
This is stronger than one particular library's tie-breaking choice. It does
not directly certify the deployed arithmetic, preprocessing, batching, kernels
or dispatch implementation. Input and checkpoint identity, eval semantics and
the numerical policy are therefore part of the result contract.

Production HZ/HiGHS acceptance is not a machine-checked proof of arbitrary
floating-point solver error. CROWN positives are numerical filters under a
different contract. Independent rational checking proves bounds for supplied
objects, not the network-to-HZ transformation that produced them. Direct
rational construction removes the floating F0-lowering assumption but retains
trust in upstream propagation, guards and infeasibility exclusions. Relaxing
binary factors to continuous intervals can prevent proof completion; a negative
checked lower bound is not a counterexample. The fresh ACT-only transfer's
three UNKNOWN requests show why one complete request-level proof cannot be
generalized to all policy-accepted safety results.

The source-proof follow-up makes the trusted boundary concrete rather than
eliminating it retrospectively. The old positive input98 proof still depends
on its supplied expert enclosure. A new checked construction covers represented
input containment, affine error compensation, every expert layer, factor maps,
registered route exclusions and output-LP construction. Its fresh evidence is
nonpositive or missing; the latest property-directed arm has eight nonpositive
obligations and one missing candidate. Thus the expert-layer trace is no longer
missing for the new representation, but complete positivity is.

It is invalid to combine the new source proof with the old matrices' positive
duals. These protocols also use different enclosures and gate ranges, so their
contrast does not isolate the effect of removing a trust assumption.
Declared-graph correspondence, checker execution and deployed floating semantics
remain distinct boundaries. A checked represented box is not automatically a
proof for every requested real epsilon-ball.

The bounded saved-record diagnosis leaves product and ReLU effects coupled at
unverified assignments. Residual-box correction is not pure roundoff, and a tiny
point-minus-bound difference does not establish exact feasibility or optimality.
No new isolated intervention was identified, so input98 follow-up is stopped:
no retry of property7, extra rows or changed budget. This is a decision about
research scope, not an impossibility or unsafe result. The complete record is
retained in [the source-proof history](../appendices/source_proof_history.md).

## Reproducibility and conclusion

Immutable request identities, failure retention, complete denominators and
independent structural re-audits improve traceability but are not themselves
certificates. The standard-library checker can be run without loading a model
or solver. A complete reviewer artifact additionally needs obtainable model and
input data, fixed environment instructions and a new executable request; private
server paths and unbundled weights remain limitations until that workflow is
provided and tested. A source-defined three-expert control now supplies a
no-download new-request workflow with a separate standard-library check; it is
not a substitute for distributing and rebuilding the empirical model tables.

A later real-input98 bundle closes portability for one stored conditional
proof: its isolated checker needs neither the checkpoint nor historical paths.
This does not yet distribute the entire empirical study or eliminate trusted
upstream lowering. The same-budget evidence-mode success is likewise one
postselected, single-pair development control, not a transferable coverage or
speed guarantee. Checked nonpositive bounds on the other four studied cases
remain visible in the saved-record cost/failure table.

The later general-evidence experiment on20 new convolutional inputs did not
replicate that control's positive outcome: all20 evidence requests timed out,
and neither comparator produced a positive result. Saved local prechecks expose
both nonpositive bounds and missing obligations, while deadline locations
include packaging and checking. A budget/implementation repair is therefore
not established as sufficient for positive coverage. The new cohort is closed
under its frozen rules; changing evidence scheduling would require a separately
identified engineering study, not replacement of these results.

The central conclusion is that route invariance is not necessary for proving
the output property, and complete route-conditioned obligations can expose
that problem to both HZ-based and general nonlinear backends. The completed
strong-baseline and mechanism studies do not establish an irreplaceable HZ
advantage or a successful MoE-aware three-component algorithm. The completed
primary cascade comparison has a positive point estimate but fails its
pre-registered practical-effect gate; pending radius and boundary results cannot
replace that primary decision. Same-source strict positive proofs
and cross-family competitiveness remain open; historical internal gains and
the explicit negative results are both retained.
