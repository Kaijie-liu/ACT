\subsection{RQ6: Overhead}

We measure the overhead of our unsoundness detection framework across
\emph{CBR sampling budgets} (5, 10, 20, 50)---i.e., the number of concrete
inputs sampled by CBR per network---and model sizes (Small, Medium, Large).
For each configuration, we report the mean validation time (in milliseconds)
for CBR, BBL, and their combination (complementary unsoundness check),
measured over 10 timing runs per configuration. BBL overhead is measured
at each budget but does not depend on the sampling budget, as it performs
a single forward pass with hook-based activation capture.

Table~\ref{tab:rq6-overhead} reports results across all tested configurations.
CBR time scales approximately linearly with sampling budget: for the Small
model, overhead increases from 0.50\,ms (budget=5) to 2.81\,ms (budget=50),
a 5.6$\times$ increase for a 10$\times$ budget increase. Medium and Large
models exhibit near-perfect linear scaling, with 9.8$\times$ and 10.0$\times$
increases respectively. BBL time is independent of sampling budget and grows
modestly with model size (0.09\,ms for Small, 0.13\,ms for Medium/Large),
remaining constant across all budgets for each model size.

At the default configuration (budget=20), combined overhead ranges from
1.28\,ms (Medium) to 1.57\,ms (Large). The cost breakdown shows that CBR
accounts for 91--94\% of total overhead at this budget, with BBL contributing
a small constant cost. At higher budgets (e.g., budget=50), CBR dominates
even more strongly (96--97\% of total cost), while at lower budgets
(e.g., budget=5), BBL becomes relatively more significant (15--31\% of cost).

Finally, overhead does not monotonically track parameter count. Medium
(34K params) exhibits lower CBR time than Small (2K params) at all budgets
except budget=50 (e.g., at budget=20: 1.16\,ms vs.\ 1.34\,ms). This suggests
that concrete execution cost is driven more by architectural factors---such
as input dimension (Small: 16, Medium: 64, Large: 256) and network depth---than
raw parameter count. BBL overhead, in contrast, depends primarily on the number
of audited layer boundaries and the cost of hook-based activation capture,
which plateaus between Medium and Large models (both 0.13\,ms).

\finding{Our unsoundness detection framework exhibits predictable and low
overhead: CBR cost scales linearly with sampling budget (0.29--3.59\,ms across
all configurations), while BBL adds a small constant cost (0.09--0.13\,ms)
independent of budget. At the default budget=20, total overhead is 1.28--1.57\,ms
across all model sizes.}
